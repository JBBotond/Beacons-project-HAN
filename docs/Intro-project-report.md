*Introduction*

How do you motivate children in an environment filled with intricate machines, complex tools, technical language, and images. Adding another layer in which the child audience must listen to complicated logic could diminish their their engagement and enjoyment. The client recognizes that such struggles are unnecessary. As a consequence, a different approach must be put in place—an approach that captures the bright energy and mood of kids in order to keep them engaged in the activity; moreover help familiarize them with the vision of the company Bronkhorst.

This project’s main goal is to deliver a stable, educational Puzzle Box from KidsBytes that maximizes the practical teaching of Bronkhorst to society under the specific requirements of the client, providing a discovery-based approach to the company and increasing future visits. The realization will be based on the FDRM MXCA153, with the team following an iterative prototyping process and maintaining close communication with the client, who brings a commercial vision. Work will be organized in clearly defined increments—each tested and refined in consultation—and bounded by preconditions such as fixed hardware, selected components, strict cost limits, and a twelve-week project time frame.

The report is organized to transparently guide the reader through the development journey:
• Chapter 2 summarizes the preliminary research and early decisions;
• Chapter 3 details functional requirements and describes the target system’s
main functions;
• Chapter 4 addresses technical design choices and underlying architecture;
• Chapter 5 documents the realization of both hardware and software;
• Chapter 6 presents the outcomes of prototype testing; finally,
• Chapter 7 discusses conclusions and recommendations, supporting future reflection and potential industrial roll out.
